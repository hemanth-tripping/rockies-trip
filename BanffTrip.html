01 · Moraine Lake

The shuttle is the whole trip

No private cars on Moraine Lake Road, ever. Sentinel Pass (Day 5) depends entirely on the Parks Canada Alpine Start shuttle reservation — check reservation.pc.gc.ca today if it isn't locked in.

02 · Sept 7

You land on Labour Day weekend

Your first three nights overlap the busiest long weekend left in the season. Lots fill before 7am — early starts on Day 2–3 aren't optional.

03 · Larch season

The larches will still be green

Gold larches peak the last two weeks of September. You'll get the alpine scale and Sentinel Pass itself — just not the postcard gold.

04 · Canmore side

Hwy 742 closure — check before Day 8

A June 2026 rockslide shut Hwy 742/Smith-Dorrien Trail indefinitely. Ha Ling's trailhead (Day 8) needs a Hwy 40 South detour if it's still closed — check albertaparks.ca the night before.

Also worth knowing: Banff National Park entry is free for everyone June 19 – Sept 7, 2026, so your first three park-days cost nothing. From Sept 8 onward you'll need a paid day pass or Discovery Pass. Day 9's Prairie View Lookout needs a separate Kananaskis Conservation Pass.

Why it's ordered this way

  • Cochrane covers Banff town on arrival day and Johnston Canyon the next — no detour, since Day 2 was headed to Banff anyway.
  • Golden anchors your two biggest hike days plus the full Icefields Parkway as its own dedicated day.
  • Golden → Radium runs through Yoho and down the Kootenay Parkway, ending with a sunset soak.
  • Harvie Heights sits right at Banff's east gate — closer to Banff townsite than Canmore itself — closing the trip with Ha Ling, Quarry Lake, and an easy Prairie View Lookout stop on the way to the airport.
